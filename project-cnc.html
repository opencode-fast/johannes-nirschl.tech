CNC Machine Build

HARDWARE FABRICATION 2025

Building a 3-axis CNC machine from scratch was an exercise in understanding the physical limits of digital fabrication. The goal was not just to assemble parts, but to design a machine capable of milling aluminum while relying entirely on open-source hardware controllers.

The Framework

I started with a rigid aluminum extrusion frame, specifically opting for C-beam profiles for the X and Y axes to minimize deflection. All custom mounting plates were initially 3D printed in PETG to test tolerances, and eventually milled out of 6mm aluminum once the machine could bootstrap itself.

Electronics & Control

The brain of the operation is an Arduino running GRBL, communicating via high-torque NEMA 23 stepper motors. Cable management was solved using drag chains to keep the workspace clean and safe.

The Result

The final build achieves a repeatable tolerance of 0.05mm. It is now a core tool in my maker space, allowing me to prototype PCBs and machine custom mechanical parts for future projects.
